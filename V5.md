# Coastline Rush v5 — Extended Odyssey

The six-world expedition is now a sustained challenge. Short, almost constant-speed stages have been replaced by longer worlds with continuous acceleration and four encounter phases.

## Stage lengths and speed

| World | Distance | Standard speed, start → finish | Expert speed, start → finish | Standard time | Expert time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| The Departure | 2,200m | 17 → 29m/s | 20 → 35m/s | 98s | 82s |
| Orbital Causeway | 2,500m | 19 → 32m/s | 22 → 38m/s | 100s | 85s |
| Mars Outpost | 2,750m | 20 → 34m/s | 23 → 40m/s | 104s | 90s |
| Verdant Nexus | 2,900m | 21 → 35m/s | 24 → 41m/s | 106s | 91s |
| Cryo Cathedral | 3,000m | 22 → 36m/s | 25 → 42m/s | 106s | 92s |
| The Star Forge | 3,300m | 23 → 38m/s | 26 → 44m/s | 110s | 96s |

Times are simulated uninterrupted base-speed travel, rounded to the nearest second, excluding portal transit, revives and temporary Air Dash. A full Standard route is about 10½ minutes including six portal transits, compared with roughly three minutes previously. Speed follows distance so restoring a checkpoint preserves its position on the difficulty curve. Entry into a new world briefly eases the pace while introducing its changed scenery and physics; later worlds have higher entry and peak speeds.

## Four phases inside every world

- **Warm-up, 0–20%:** introduce the world's movement rules, required jumps/slides and lane reading.
- **Pressure, 20–45%:** tighter encounter intervals and the first compound lane sequences.
- **Intense, 45–72%:** more mixed jump/slide choices, roof transfers and hazards around landing zones.
- **Overdrive, 72–100%:** the fastest speed, frequent compound encounters and the smallest allowed recovery windows, followed by a protected portal approach.

Eight encounter families are selected using the seeded generator instead of a short repeating row cycle: full-width jumps, full-width slides, jump/slide/blocked lane choices, double lane weaves, cargo or rover roof transfers, charging cargo, jumps with landing hazards, and mixed two-beat sequences. Later phases favor compound patterns. World-specific hazards remain: orbital gaps, Mars meteors and moving rovers, alien roots and lasers, cracked ice, and forge sentinels.

Encounter placement uses expected speed and reaction time. Orbit retains enough time to complete its long low-gravity flight; ice retains a wider steering window. Moving cargo starts charging on its final approach. Optional second roofs leave a safe landing route. The final clear stretch scales with peak speed, providing more than three seconds to recover and read the portal. Relics are spread near 25%, 51% and 77% of each stage.

In a five-seed generated-course density sample, Standard late-stage hazards per second increased by about 75–95% in five worlds and 23% in Orbit. These are measured sample averages, not a claim about every seed or human reaction time.

## HUD and progress

The live HUD now shows difficulty phase, speed in km/h, and distance against the longer stage target. Crossing into a new phase announces the increase in speed and hazards. Existing controls, artifacts, precision portals, mastery, daily runs and ghost racing remain available.

## Existing saves

- Wallet, unlocks, relic collection and earned medals are retained.
- An interrupted v4 run finishes its current stage with its original length, speed and generator; its next stage adopts v5. This avoids suddenly moving its portal or adding obstacles around the player.
- New runs use v5 immediately. Start a fresh Odyssey to play the entire updated route.
- Short-stage time and score records are retained in an archived save field and kept separate from new-course records. A resumed old-stage completion also goes to that archive.
- Old Odyssey ghosts are incompatible with the longer route and are discarded. Endless, Sprint and Jet Ski ghosts remain compatible.
